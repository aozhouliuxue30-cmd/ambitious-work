# Example: Turn feedback notes into a project brief

[中文](project-brief.md) | [English](project-brief.en.md)

Version: 1.1.0

These are fictional teaching materials. Copy the entire input below. No browsing or attachment features are required.

## Copyable input

```text
Output language: [中文 / English / 中文 + English]
Use Ambitious Work to turn the following feedback into a project brief for design peers.
Do not stop at a plan. Deliver the brief first, then an evidence check. Keep the English
version to 450 words or fewer; for bilingual output apply this limit to the English portion.

Project: A registration form for a book exchange event.
Materials:
[A] Trial record: Participant A paused at “exchange preferences” on a phone and asked whether it was required.
[B] Trial record: Participant B submitted successfully but did not know when to expect confirmation.
[C] Organizer note: All fields are currently required. There is no actual registration data yet.
[D] Current process: After submission, the page only says “Submitted successfully.” The organizer plans to reply manually each week.

Constraints: These are two trial submissions, not a representative sample. There is no
timing, conversion, or actual registration data. Do not claim to have identified causes
of abandonment or promise automatic notifications on the organizer's behalf.

The deliverable must contain the main problems, source labels for each judgment,
at least one alternative explanation, an actionable next step, and one hypothesis still
to test. Preserve the current reality of manual replies.
```

## Expected output structure

1. **Project brief:** Explain the issues and limits using the supplied materials.
2. **Evidence table:** Judgment, source labels, and what the evidence can support.
3. **Next steps:** Changes and specific copy possible within the current process.
4. **Open questions:** Hypotheses, follow-up observations, and conclusions that cannot be drawn from two trials.

## Acceptable reference excerpt

> The two trials exposed unclear required-field guidance and unclear post-submission expectations. [A][C] support reviewing the “exchange preferences” instructions; [B][D] support explaining how and when replies happen. These materials cannot establish registration abandonment or estimate its frequency.
>
> First discuss whether “exchange preferences” must be required. The confirmation page could say: “We received your registration. The organizer will review and reply manually each week.” The reply channel still needs the organizer's confirmation and should not be invented.

## What to check

- Two trials are not described as large-scale research, and no conversion rate is invented.
- “Participant A paused to ask” does not become “Participant A abandoned registration.”
- Include an alternative explanation, such as difficulty understanding the question itself.
- Distinguish copy changes possible now from follow-up tests that have not run.
- In bilingual output, preserve [A]–[D], the number of trials, manual replies, and uncertainty in both languages.

This reference structure and excerpt were authored as acceptance guidance with AI assistance. They are not outputs from a completed model test. The English length limit adapts the Chinese example's 700-character limit for readability; the fictional source facts are unchanged.
