# Example: Turn a learning idea into a small experiment

[中文](learning-experiment.md) | [English](learning-experiment.en.md)

Version: 1.1.0

These are fictional teaching materials for an ordinary text conversation. No software installation is required.

## Copyable input

```text
Output language: [中文 / English / 中文 + English]
Use Ambitious Work to help me start learning interview questioning, rather than only
recommending a learning path. I have 30 minutes today. Do not contact real participants,
register accounts, or pay for anything.

Practice context: Understand how classmates organize registration for a club event.
My three questions:
1. Don't you think the current registration process is a hassle?
2. If we built a new system, would you like it?
3. You want the system to be simple and fast, right?

Deliver now:
- Revised versions of all three questions, each with one sentence explaining the change;
- A 10-minute interview outline with opening, main questions, follow-ups, and closing;
- A 30-minute practice schedule I can do today, with a stopping condition.

Constraints: Keep the event registration topic. Do not treat simulated answers as actual interview data.
Acceptance criteria: Questions do not presuppose the answers and can elicit specific
past experiences. The first version must be ready to read and use.
Mark it as untested in real interviews; do not claim user research has been completed.
```

## Expected output structure

1. **Question revision table:** Original question, revised question, and reason.
2. **10-minute outline:** Timed opening, questions, follow-ups, and closing.
3. **30-minute practice:** Reading, rewriting, reading aloud, and checking, producing one outline.
4. **Status:** A first version has been written; real interviews and validation have not occurred.

## Acceptable reference excerpt

> Revised question: “Think back to the last time you organized registration for a club event. What did you do from preparation through confirming the registrations?”
>
> Reason: Replace the assumption that the process is a hassle with a question about a specific experience, allowing the participant to describe the process first.
>
> Possible follow-ups: “What happened at that point?” “How did you handle it afterward?” Do not record sample answers as participant quotes.

## What to check

- All three original questions are revised, with no request to agree with an existing judgment.
- The interview outline totals 10 minutes and the practice schedule totals 30 minutes.
- Today's output is a usable outline, not conclusions from research that has not occurred.
- Stop after 30 minutes with a first version. Do not automatically book interviews, create accounts, or pay.
- In bilingual output, preserve the three questions, both time limits, and the unverified status in both languages.

This reference structure and excerpt were authored as acceptance guidance with AI assistance. They are not outputs from a completed model test.
